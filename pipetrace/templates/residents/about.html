{% translate "What is a service line?" %}

{% translate "It's the pipe that carries water from the water main under the street into a building. Part of it usually belongs to the utility and part to the property owner. Many homes built before the 1960s were connected with lead pipe." %}

{% translate "Why is there an inventory?" %}

{% blocktranslate with deadline=site.replacement_deadline|date:"DATE_FORMAT" %}Federal rules (the Lead and Copper Rule Improvements) require every community water system to list every service line, say whether it is lead, and make that list public. The first inventories were due October 16, 2024. Lead lines, and galvanized lines that need replacing, must be replaced by {{ deadline }}.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% translate "Many service lines were installed a century ago, and records were lost or never kept. Unknown doesn't mean lead, but it might be. Checking the pipe yourself and sending us a photo is the quickest way to find out." %}

{% translate "Is my water safe to drink?" %}

{% translate "Water usually leaves the treatment plant without lead. Lead gets in from lead pipes, lead solder and older brass fixtures, and it varies from tap to tap and day to day. There is no known safe level of lead exposure, so if your line is lead, galvanized or unknown, use a filter certified to remove lead for drinking and cooking water." %}
